The [Perl and Raku Foundation](https://www.perlfoundation.org/) is delighted to announce that [HeroDevs](https://www.herodevs.com/) has donated $10,000 to support our work. We're grateful for their generosity — gifts like this go directly toward the people and infrastructure that keep Perl and CPAN healthy.

HeroDevs provides long-term security and support for open source software that has reached its end of life.

As we continue to see support from community members and corporate sponsors,
2026 has been shaping up to be a genuinely good year for Perl and CPAN.

The [CPAN Security Group](https://security.metacpan.org/)
has been very busy with security research, publishing CVEs and patching some of the
exploits which they have found.
[Alpha-Omega](https://alpha-omega.dev/) [funded that
work](https://www.perl.com/article/alpha-omega-donates-usd-250-000-for-perl-and-cpan-security/),
which was a solid vote of confidence in the ecosystem. The CPAN Security Group is also working
with other packaging ecosystems to learn from their experiences fighting the
same battles. This is the kind of work that cannot be done solely by
volunteers. We would like to see it funded on an ongoing basis.

Meanwhile, the ecosystem itself is growing. [Comparing January to August 2026
against the same period in
2025](https://www.olafalders.com/2026/09/04/cpan-uploads-up-50-percent-year-over-year/),
we can see significant growth in CPAN across multiple metrics. The first-time
releaser number is particularly encouraging. It's great to see people deciding
to publish on CPAN for the first time.

- First-time releasers: 31 to 54, up 74%
- New distributions: 464 to 1,007, more than double
- Uploads: 6,276 to 9,430, up 50% — already past last year's full-year total with four months to go
- Distinct authors releasing: 489 to 546, up 12%

The in-person side has been busy as well. The [Perl Toolchain
Summit](https://www.perl.com/article/perl-toolchain-summit-2026-key-results/)
met in beautiful Vienna, Austria in April to work on the infrastructure everyone depends
on and nobody sees. The [Perl and Raku
Conference](https://tprc.us/tprc-2026-gsp/) ran in Greenville, South Carolina
in June, and the [London Perl & Raku Workshop](https://act.yapc.eu/lpw2026/) is coming
up in the UK at the end of November. Perl 5.44 also shipped in July — the
annual stable release, succeeding 5.42 (odd versions like 5.41 and 5.43 being
development-only).

It has been a good year, but there's plenty still to do and we need your help to do it.

If your organization is interested in becoming a TPRF donor, please [reach out
to me](mailto:olaf@perlfoundation.org). I'd be more than happy to set up a
call so that we can discuss how a donor relationship with TPRF can
benefit your organization.
